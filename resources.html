Resources We Use & Recommend

Every printable in our shop is made to be printed at home, simply. These are the kinds of supplies we reach for ourselves and point our customers to — nothing fancy, nothing you need a degree to use. Our own planners and games always come first; everything else here is the supporting cast.

Printing at home

All-in-one inkjet printer — prints, scans and copies in one machine. For printables, the thing that matters most is cheap ink per page, not the price of the printer itself. Look for one that happily takes thicker paper.

Card stock (65 lb or heavier) — the secret to games that feel like real games. Bingo cards, game pieces and invitations printed on card stock survive the whole party and can be kept for next year.

Everyday printer paper — regular paper is perfect for planner pages, answer sheets and anything you'll write on and use up. Slightly brighter white paper makes coloring pages look their best.

Printer ink — keep a spare set in the drawer. Parties have a way of landing on the same weekend your cartridge runs dry.

Paper trimmer — a small sliding trimmer gives straight, clean edges on cards and game pieces in seconds, with no ruler-and-scissors wobble.

Laminator and pouches — optional, but lovely for games you'll reuse: laminate Bingo cards once and play with dry-erase markers year after year.

Party hosting basics

Pens and pencils, a big mixed pack — every trivia and word game needs one per guest, and they wander off. Buy more than you think you need; it's the cheapest insurance a host can buy.

Clipboards — not everyone gets a seat at the table. A clipboard turns any lap into a writing desk for answer sheets and scavenger hunts.

Small prizes — a little bag of treats, candles or gift cards for the winners. Games get twice as lively when there's something — anything — to win.

Clear storage folders or large envelopes — print each game, slip it in a folder with its answer key, and next year's party is already half planned.

Budgeting tools

Start with the planner itself — paper budgeting only sticks if the page in front of you is simple. Ours are the ones we recommend first, because we made them for beginners:

All 12 Budget Planners Bundle — every planner we make in one download: monthly, paycheck, savings, debt, bills and more. The best place to start, and the best value.

2027 Budget Planner Bundle — a fresh, dated planner to carry you through the whole of next year.

Then a few simple supplies turn loose pages into a system:

3-ring binder — one binder for the whole year of planner pages. Kitchen-table budgeting works because everything lives in one place.

Tab dividers — a tab for each planner (budget, bills, savings, debt) so your five-minute weekly check-in really takes five minutes.

Cash envelopes — for the spending categories that always creep (groceries, eating out, fun money). When the envelope is empty, the category is done — simple and honest.

Smooth-writing pens you actually like — a small thing that matters more than it should: a pen you enjoy makes the weekly check-in feel like a treat, not a chore.
